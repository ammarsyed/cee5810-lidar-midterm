# Decisions made while building

Calls made so the build could keep going. None of them blocks anything. Each one says what it is in plain English, the tradeoff, and a recommendation. **Status** is *open* until one of us confirms it. To change one, edit the code location listed, rerun `uv run python -m experiments.run_all --person <you>`, and update this file.

**Mention in the report:** #1, #2, #7 and #9. A grader could reasonably ask about them.

| # | Decision (plain English) | Tradeoff | Recommendation | Where | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mirrors and glass reflect from **both sides**. Real mirrors have a painted, non-reflective back. | Simpler code vs. realism. In our rooms a beam almost never reaches the back of the mirror, so results don't change. | **Keep**; list it as a limitation. | `lidar_sim/materials.py` (add a one-sided flag to change) | open |
| 2 | How bright a **head-on mirror/glass reflection** is: factor `K_s/K = 1/(8θ_w²) ≈ 182`. *Corrected from 730* after the math review. | 730 broke energy conservation: the mirror sent back 4× more light than it received. 182 follows from the derivation (D5) and keeps the model physical. | **Keep 182**; it's the defensible value. | `lidar_sim/config.py` (`SPEC_GAIN`) | open |
| 3 | The default room's mirror is tilted **45°**, not facing the room. variant_b's mirror is at 135°. | Facing the room, bounced light travels ~7 m, past the sensor's 5.6 m range, so you'd see **zero** ghost points. Tilted, reflections hit a nearby wall and ghosts appear within ~3 m of the mirror. | **Keep.** "Ghosts only show up close to a mirror" is itself a result. | `lidar_sim/scenes.py` (`mirror_angle_deg`) | open |
| 4 | Each beam makes **one** detect/miss decision, on its strongest echo. | A real sensor might fall back to a weaker second echo. One decision per beam keeps the simulator and the particle filter's math identical (exactly D6). | **Keep.** | `lidar_sim/sensor.py` (`chosen_returns`) | open |
| 5 | Glass is visible only within **~4° of head-on** (4.3° for glass 2 m away). | Not really a choice: it follows from #2 and real glass physics. It matches what the Heiden paper saw with a plastic sheet. | **Keep.** | follows from #2 | open |
| 6 | **Videos are not stored in git.** | Keeps the repo small (one GIF was 13 MB). Anyone who clones must run `run_all` (~4.5 min) to get the videos. | **Keep**; upload videos to Canvas directly. | `.gitignore` | open |
| 7 | The particle filter assumes **5 cm** range noise when weighting particles; the real noise is 1 cm. | With 1 cm and 300 particles, one particle grabs all the weight and the filter breaks. The alternatives are many more particles (~10× slower) or a deliberately blurrier likelihood. The blur is standard practice (Thrun et al.). | **Keep**; one sentence in the report. | `experiments/e11_localization.py` (`SIGMA_LIK`) | open |
| 8 | **No ICP comparison** (a classic scan-matching algorithm the Heiden paper compares against). | ~1 hour of extra work for one more baseline figure. The three-filter comparison already shows the main point. | **Skip**, unless there's spare time after the reports. | would be `lidar_sim/localization/icp.py` | open |
| 9 | The localization corridor is **14 m long**, with objects behind a glass wall. | At 10 m the end walls were always in range, so the glass barely mattered. At 14 m there's a stretch where the only position information is *behind the glass*. It's a deliberately designed stress test. | **Keep**, and report both scenes honestly: big win in the corridor (11.3 vs 0.9 cm), small win in the plain room (0.98 vs 0.70 cm). | `lidar_sim/scenes.py` (`glass_corridor(length=…)`) | open |
| 10 | The notebook is saved **without outputs**; its HTML export is regenerated, not committed; the interactive cell is off by default; the notebook runs E11 with 3 seeds instead of 20. | Small repo and a fast notebook. The 20-seed numbers come from the script. | **Keep**; regenerate the HTML right before submitting (~25 s). | `notebooks/make_notebook.py`, `.gitignore` | open |
| 11 | **One config file per person** (`people/ammar.toml`, `people/gal.toml`): room, random seed, output folder, and every experiment's poses and parameters. Shared physics (sensor, materials, noise) is the same for both. | The assignment needs distinct media from each person. Varying only the *experimental setup* keeps one consistent model and one set of derivations, while every output file differs. Charts of the same experiment have the same shape but different content and numbers. | **Keep.** Each person writes up their own numbers. If you want *more* difference, edit your own `.toml` (e.g. other reflectivities or poses) and rerun. | `people/*.toml`, `experiments/common.py` | open |
